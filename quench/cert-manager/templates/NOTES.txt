Quenchworks cert-manager is installed as {{ include "quench-common.fullname" . }}.

It runs three deployments from the four hardened cert-manager images:

  {{ include "cert-manager.controllerName" . }}   reconcile loop + ACME (launches acmesolver pods on demand)
  {{ include "cert-manager.webhookName" . }}      validating + mutating admission webhook (:443 -> {{ .Values.webhook.securePort }})
  {{ include "cert-manager.cainjectorName" . }}   CA-bundle injection into the webhook configs

No external cert-manager is required: the webhook self-bootstraps its serving CA
into the Secret {{ include "cert-manager.webhookCASecretName" . }}, and the
cainjector injects that CA into the Validating/Mutating webhook configurations.

The CRDs (Certificate, Issuer, ClusterIssuer, CertificateRequest, Order,
Challenge) are installed from the chart's crds/ directory.

Try it with a SelfSigned issuer (no external CA needed):

  kubectl apply -f - <<'EOF'
  apiVersion: cert-manager.io/v1
  kind: ClusterIssuer
  metadata:
    name: selfsigned
  spec:
    selfSigned: {}
  ---
  apiVersion: cert-manager.io/v1
  kind: Certificate
  metadata:
    name: example
    namespace: {{ .Release.Namespace }}
  spec:
    secretName: example-tls
    issuerRef:
      name: selfsigned
      kind: ClusterIssuer
    commonName: example.local
    dnsNames:
      - example.local
  EOF

  kubectl wait --for=condition=Ready certificate/example -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} --timeout=120s
  kubectl get secret example-tls -n {{ .Release.Namespace }}   # populated tls.crt / tls.key

The images are pinned by digest and signed. Verify, e.g. the controller:

  cosign verify ghcr.io/quenchworks/images/cert-manager-controller \
    --certificate-identity-regexp 'https://github.com/quenchworks/.+' \
    --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com
